A 23% lift I refused to call causal

A Round · consumer onboarding
What felt off

We asked people to describe their dating preferences in writing, at the exact moment they had the least patience and the least investment.

What I did

Scored 10 opportunities down to 3, shipped a visual taxonomy, and defined the metrics and analytics schema before the rollout.

What the evidence said

Completion rose 52% to 64%. But p ≈ 0.24 and it wasn't randomized, so I called it directional and asked for a real experiment.

The eval that killed my roadmap

Kapi · agent built end to end
What felt off

The agent I'd built demoed beautifully, which bothered me, because every AI product demos beautifully when you're the one driving.

What I did

Wrote a 51-case labeled eval scoring competence and honesty separately, then attributed every failure to a component.

What the evidence said

19 model-side against 4 retrieval-side. I'd spent weeks on retrieval. It was fine. I was wrong, and I changed direction.

Rebuilding trust in an AI agent

independent research + prototype
What felt off

An agent I pay for filed applications on my behalf, and the output started saying things about me I couldn't defend in a room.

What I did

Logged 350+ applications with dates and screenshots, attributed each failure to a pipeline stage, then built the gate that stops it.

What the evidence said

Every failure was invisible to the metric being optimized. The metric explained the failures, so I replaced it and instrumented the replacement.

What does "works on Windows" actually mean?

Digital Robotics · MVP definition
What felt off

Everyone agreed on the goal and nobody agreed on what done meant. Engineering, users, and our own demo team each had a different answer.

What I did

Defined the MVP around two user groups and moved the bar from "the software runs" to "a target user can install, launch, recover, and relaunch it alone."

What the evidence said

The win wasn't the 7-second launch. It was that demos stopped needing an engineer in the room.

What I'm looking for

/

A full-time product role where the question is still open. I'm most useful early, when nobody has decided yet what the product is supposed to be good at, because that decision is the one everything else inherits.

The thread through all four cases is the same. A product has to be trusted before anyone can use it, and usually nobody has defined what trusted means or how you would know. That was true for clinical assessment models, for sourcing decisions with real money behind them, and it's true now for agents that act while you aren't watching.

I try to be careful about what I claim. Two of the cases above include a section on what the evidence does not support, which is deliberate. A number you can't defend in a room is worse than no number.
