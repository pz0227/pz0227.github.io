AI Product Teardowns

Independent research series · ongoing

Teardowns of AI products I actually pay for, built from logged daily usage rather than demo tours. One question runs through the series. As AI products act for users instead of just answering them, where does trust break, and what would a PM measure and build to fix it?

  • Flagship case built from 350+ real applications through an AI job-search agent
  • 20+ documented failures, each dated and attributed to the pipeline stage that caused it
  • Proposed Trusted Qualified Applications as a replacement north star, with four instrumented proxies
  • Every claim labeled observed, calculated, inferred, or hypothesis

Trust Layer

Interactive prototype · independent concept

The diagnosis turned into a product. A pre-submission gate built on one rule: critical facts are read from your verified profile, never generated. Try to submit an invented resume claim and watch the gate respond.

  • Read-never-generated policy for identity facts like employer, salary, and work authorization
  • Resume diff with a one-tap acknowledgment before anything carrying your name goes out
  • Designed around the tradeoff that actually matters here, speed against trust

Kapi

AI product analytics agent · built end to end

An agent that answers product questions in plain language, built with retrieval and LLM APIs. Then I wrote the evaluation suite, mostly because I wanted a number I could defend in a room. The eval did not confirm my roadmap. It killed it.

  • 51-case labeled evaluation suite covering answer quality and appropriate refusal
  • I had assumed retrieval was the bottleneck. Fault attribution came out 19 model-side against 4 retrieval-side
  • The real problem was answering confidently when it should have refused, which is a different roadmap entirely

About

I studied psychology at UC Santa Barbara and then statistics, measurement, and assessment at the University of Pennsylvania. Those two things do different jobs for me. Psychology is why every teardown starts with something that simply feels off. Statistics is why that feeling does not count until it survives evidence.

What I keep ending up in is the same problem wearing different clothes. A product has to be trusted before anyone can use it, and nobody has decided what trusted means or how you would know. That was true for clinical models, for sourcing decisions with real money behind them, and it is true now for agents that act while you are not watching.
