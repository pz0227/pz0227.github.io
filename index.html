How I think about product

/

Psychology at UC Santa Barbara, then statistics and measurement at Penn. Those do different jobs for me. Psychology is why every teardown starts with something that simply feels off. Statistics is why that feeling does not count until it survives evidence.

The belief underneath all four cases above is the same. Most AI products cannot tell you when they are wrong, and the metric on the dashboard usually cannot either, because the action completed. It just completed wrong. Deciding what "good" means, before you build, is the product decision. Everything after that is execution.

I also try to be careful about what I claim. Two of the cases here include a section on what the evidence does not support. That is deliberate. A number you cannot defend in a room is worse than no number.
